Reviewing threats and scoring risk#

Each generated threat comes with content you read and fields you fill in. This page explains both, and gives a review routine that works.

What each threat tells you#

Field Content
Title Names the real source and target, for example "Spoofing of Engineering Workstation towards Line 3 PLC"
Description What could happen and why
Priority High, Medium or Low
Category STRIDE
Possible Mitigation(s) / Recommended Countermeasure(s) Controls that address it
MITRE ATT&CK for ICS Related techniques (v20 IDs)
IEC 62443 Requirement(s) 4-2 CR/EDR/HDR/NDR/SAR, 3-3 SR, 2-1, 2-4, 3-2 or IEC 61511 references
Foundational Requirement FR1 to FR7
Relevant from SL Lowest SL-T at which countermeasures are normally required
OT Consequence Safety, environment, equipment, production, view or control, IP

Fields you fill in#

Product and System template Plant and Site template
Likelihood (1-5) Existing Countermeasures
Impact (1-5) HAZOP or Cyber HAZOP Reference
Risk Rating Consequence Category
Risk Treatment Consequence Severity (1-5) and Likelihood (1-5)
Implemented Mitigation(s) Current Risk and Within Tolerable Risk
Verification Evidence Risk Treatment, Planned Countermeasure(s) and Residual Risk
Owner Action Owner, Due Date, Verification Evidence

Risk matrix#

Risk = likelihood × impact (or consequence severity), on a 5 × 5 matrix.

Score Rating
20-25 Critical
12-19 High
6-11 Medium
1-5 Low

Treatment options are mitigate, transfer, avoid or accept. Record accepted risks together with the owner who accepted them.

A review routine that works#

  1. Go flow by flowSort the Threat List by Interaction and walk the diagram one flow at a time.
  2. Fix properties, not threatsIf a threat cannot apply because of something you forgot to answer, such as "this device has no web interface", correct the property. The threat then disappears for good, and also from the next model built on it.
  3. Mark the restUse Not Applicable with a one-line justification, or Mitigated with the controls named.
  4. Score and treatFill in likelihood, impact and treatment for what remains.
  5. Check against your SL-TSort by Relevant from SL. Everything at or below your target SL should be mitigated or explicitly accepted.

Do I need CVSS?#

No. CVSS scores known vulnerabilities in products. A threat model looks at design-level threats before any vulnerability exists, so it uses likelihood × impact (or consequence severity), as IEC 62443-3-2 does. CVSS becomes useful later, when a specific vulnerability (a CVE) is found in a component you modelled.
