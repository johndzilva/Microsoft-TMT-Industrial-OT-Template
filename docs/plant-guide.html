Plant and site risk assessment#

The method for an asset owner assessing an operating site, following the IEC 62443-3-2 zone and conduit requirements (ZCR), using the OT Plant and Site Risk Assessment template.

What the plant threats cover#

  • Zones and conduits: unfiltered flows between zones, unauthenticated control traffic, paths from the business network or the Internet to controllers, unmanaged cellular routers, wireless.
  • Remote and third-party access: stolen remote access credentials, always-on vendor connections, remote takeover of operator sessions, integrators working without security requirements.
  • Assets: unpatched and end-of-life systems, missing malware protection, unhardened configuration, shared and leftover accounts, no monitoring, no tested backup, capability below the zone target (SL-C < SL-T), ransomware spread.
  • Operations and process: attacks that drive the process to a hazardous state, unmanaged logic changes, safety system compromise and SIS independence, loss of view without manual fallback, alarm suppression, permissive firewall rules, IT scanning of fragile devices.
  • People, media and data: USB media and visiting laptops, phishing, insider misuse, untraceable actions, theft of plant documents, backups encrypted together with the plant, unreviewed alerts, physical access at unattended sites, cloud service compromise.

References use IEC 62443-2-1:2024 programme elements (for example 2-1 NET 3 secure remote access), IEC 62443-2-4 service provider capabilities (2-4 SP.07), IEC 62443-3-2 (3-2 ZCR 5), IEC 62443-3-3 system requirements, and IEC 61511-1 clause 8.2.4.

Step by step#

Step IEC 62443-3-2 What to do in TMT
1. System under consideration ZCR 1 Draw the site's assets with the same stencils as the product template. Include the people and the external connections: vendors, enterprise IT, cloud, laptops, USB media.
2. Initial risk ZCR 2 Note the worst-case consequence of each zone in the zone properties.
3. Zones and conduits ZCR 3 Draw each zone as a border boundary, and connect zones only through flows that really exist. A firewall or data diode between zones is a component (NDR stencil) on the path.
4. Zone properties ZCR 4, 6 Set the Purdue level, zone SL-T, worst-case consequence, safety or environmental functions, physical access and achieved SL (SL-A). These record the inputs; they do not change the threats.
5. Asset and flow properties ZCR 5 For each asset, set its site state: patch status, hardening, malware protection, shared accounts, monitoring, tested backup, SL-C meets zone SL-T, worst-case consequence. For each flow: protocol security, filtered by a firewall or conduit device, permanent connection. These decide which threats appear.
6. Detailed risk assessment ZCR 5 In Analysis View, record for each threat the existing countermeasures, consequence severity, likelihood, current risk, whether it is within tolerable risk, the treatment, planned countermeasures and residual risk.
7. Document and track ZCR 6, 7 Create the full report. Use Action Owner, Due Date and Verification Evidence to track the treatment plan.

Safety instrumented systems#

If the site has a SIS, the plant threats also cover the IEC 61511-1 clause 8.2.4 security risk assessment: SIS independence, remote reset of the ESD, and connections to the safety network.

  • Put the SIS in its own Safety Zone SIS.
  • Mark safety controllers and safety I/O as safety-related. Their stencils already do this.
  • Draw hard-wired trip inputs with Physical Medium = Hardwired signal. They then carry the field wiring threat (PL-T11) instead of network threats.
  • Link each threat to the process hazard through the HAZOP or Cyber HAZOP Reference field.

Combining with a product model#

If a supplier has modelled the product with the product template, take its capability security level (SL-C) and answer Component Capability (SL-C) Meets Zone SL-T on the asset. A No brings in the SL gap threat, so the gap is recorded and treated.
