Threat modeling basics#

Threat modeling is a structured way to find security problems in a design before an attacker does. This page covers what you need before you draw your first OT model.

Four questions#

Every threat model answers four questions:

  1. What are we working on? A diagram of the system and how data moves through it.
  2. What can go wrong? The threats against each part of the diagram.
  3. What are we going to do about it? Mitigations, requirements, or an accepted risk.
  4. Did we do a good job? Review, testing, and keeping the model up to date.

The Threat Modeling Tool helps with the first two: you draw the diagram and the template generates the threats. The other two are your review, which you record in TMT against each threat.

The data flow diagram#

A threat model is drawn as a data flow diagram (DFD). It shows how data moves, not how cables are laid.

Element Shape in TMT OT examples
Process: runs code Circle PLC, RTU, safety controller, HMI application, SCADA server, firewall, update service
External interactor: outside your control Rectangle Operator, engineer, vendor support, enterprise IT, the physical process, a USB stick
Data store: data at rest Two parallel lines Control logic, configuration, firmware image, key store, historian, audit log
Data flow: data moving between two elements Arrow Modbus TCP, DNP3, OPC UA, PROFINET, MQTT, a hard-wired trip signal, a firmware download
Trust boundary: where trust changes Dashed box or line IEC 62443 zone, conduit, DMZ, device boundary, Internet, physical access boundary

Draw the boundaries carefully

Attacks happen where data crosses a trust boundary. A flow that enters the control zone from the enterprise network is where most of the important threats appear.

STRIDE#

Every threat has one STRIDE category:

Threat Violates OT example
S Spoofing Authenticity A rogue device sends Modbus writes pretending to be the HMI
T Tampering Integrity Controller logic is changed through the engineering protocol
R Repudiation Non-repudiation A setpoint change cannot be traced to a person
I Information disclosure Confidentiality Project files with network details and passwords are copied
D Denial of service Availability A malformed packet stops a protection relay
E Elevation of privilege Authorisation A vendor remote session reaches the safety system

What makes OT different#

  • Safety and the physical process come first. The worst outcome is not a data breach but an explosion, a spill or a blackout. Each threat states its OT consequence, and the plant template scores consequence severity.
  • Zones and conduits. IEC 62443 groups assets into zones with a target security level (SL-T, 1 to 4) and connects them through conduits. You draw zones as boundaries.
  • Long lifetimes and legacy. Controllers run for 20 years, often unpatched, on protocols with no authentication. Properties such as Legacy or Unsupported Platform and Protocol Security in Use = None bring these threats in.
  • Two kinds of assessment. A supplier asks whether a product is secure by design (IEC 62443-4-2 components, 3-3 systems). An asset owner asks whether a running site is secure enough (IEC 62443-3-2). That is why there are two templates.

Which template to use#

You are… Use The threats are about
Designing or certifying a product (IEC 62443-4-2) or a system (IEC 62443-3-3) OT Product and System Design: authentication, firmware integrity, secure boot, protocol robustness, the build pipeline
Assessing an operating plant or site (IEC 62443-3-2) OT Plant and Site Risk Assessment Operations: patching, remote and vendor access, segmentation, backups, monitoring, people, removable media and the process consequences
Doing both Both Model the product first. Its capability security level (SL-C) then becomes an input to the plant assessment, where it is compared with the zone's SL-T.

Security levels in one minute#

Term Meaning Where it appears
SL-T (target) The level a zone or component must reach, from the risk assessment Zone property, component property (record only)
SL-C (capability) The level a product can reach when configured correctly, from the supplier Plant property SL-C Meets Zone SL-T
SL-A (achieved) The level a zone actually reaches today Zone property in the plant template
Relevant from SL The lowest SL at which a threat's countermeasures are normally required Threat field

SL 1 protects against casual or accidental violation. SL 2 protects against intentional attacks with simple means. SL 3 protects against sophisticated means with IACS-specific skills. SL 4 protects against the same, with extended resources.
